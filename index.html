History!

Amadeo Avagadro hypothesized in the year 1811 that the volume of a gas (at a given pressure and temperature) is proportional to the number of atoms or molecules

Advances in gas laws and kinetic theory by Josef Loschmidt in 1865 led a numberical estimate of the number of particles per cubic centimeter of gas at standard temperature and pressure, now called the Loschmidt constant.

The concept of a mole wasn't solidified until 1860 at the Karlsruhe Congress, where Karlsruhe Congress presented a method for determining atomic weights, which was based on Avagadro's hypothesis.

In the early 1900s, Jean Perrin provided the first acceptable value through Brownian motion experiments, and named the constant after Avagadro
